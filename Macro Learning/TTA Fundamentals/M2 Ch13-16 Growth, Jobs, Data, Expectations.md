# TTA · Ch 13–16 (Growth & the cycle → Jobs & rate of change → Reading the data → The expectations game)
Condensed notes in my own words. `[Claude+]` = my additions.

## Ch 13 · Growth and the Business Cycle
**13.1 GDP & the cycle**: GDP = total output value; always use **real** GDP (nominal minus inflation). Cycle phases: expansion → peak → contraction → recovery. Rough recession rule: 2 negative real quarters. Strong growth leans CBs hawkish (yields and currency up), weak growth the reverse. GDP is quarterly and late, so markets treat it as confirmation. Know the phase: the same print means different things in different phases.
**13.2 Surveys first**: PMIs = purchasing managers' better/worse answers; **50 = the line** (distance from 50 = strength). Leading and rarely revised, unlike official (lagging) data. Two surveys: manufacturing (cyclical, turns first, swings harder) and **services** (most of the economy, usually more important for the currency). Other early reads: consumer sentiment (UMich, Conference Board), where CBs mainly watch the **inflation-expectations question** (sentiment tracks actual spending only loosely); **building permits** (the most rate-sensitive leading indicator: policy rate → mortgage rates → permits → construction jobs → growth). Case: US ISM manufacturing <50 for over a year from late 2022, while GDP grew ~2.5% in 2023 and payrolls averaged ~200k/month. *A sub-50 survey = one sector's mood.*
**13.3 The consumer**: household spending ≈ two-thirds of US GDP → **retail sales** = the monthly till receipts. Loop: jobs → income → spending → revenue → hiring. Strong retail sales → yields up, currency supported; for equities it's double-edged (earnings good, rates higher).

## Ch 14 · Jobs and the Rate of Change
**14.1 Labour market**: the bridge between growth and inflation. US jobs report (first Friday): **NFP** (steady state ~+150–200k), unemployment rate (lagging), weekly **jobless claims** (the fastest data; a sustained rise warns early), **average hourly earnings** (the inflation link: ~3% compatible with 2% inflation; ≥5% isn't). Read it as a package. Single NFP prints are noise; use the **3-month average**. Case: Aug 2024 (July NFP ~115k vs ~175k expected, unemployment ~4.3%) → 2y yields collapsed, S&P fell, Nikkei −12%+ in one day. Jobs are the "last domino".
**14.2 Direction vs rate of change**: markets trade **better/worse (and how fast)** more than good/bad. Weak but improving can rally; strong but fading can worry. Turning points in surveys and claims reveal change first. Case: 2023, when inflation stayed above target all year (~3.4% at end) but kept falling → S&P +~24%, 10y from ~5% (Oct) to ~3.9% (Dec). *Direction of travel, not destination.*

## Ch 15 · Reading the Data
**15.1 Economic calendar**: previous / **consensus** / actual. Impact flags; reliably high-impact: CPI, NFP, CB decisions, GDP, PMIs, retail sales; US data matters most globally. Most US data lands at **08:30 New York** (13:30 UK / 14:30 CET). Judge the actual against consensus, not zero or the previous (e.g. inflation falling but by less than expected = a mild upside surprise).
**15.2 Surprises & "priced in"**: the reaction is to actual − consensus; in-line prints are muted. Surprise size drives move size. Direction depends on what the surprise does to expected rates.
**15.3 Good news can be bad news**: second-order chain: strong data → fewer cuts → yields up → valuations squeezed → stocks down; USD up, gold down. Soft data can rally risk (renewed cut hopes). The **mode** depends on the dominant fear: *rates* (good news = bad) vs *recession* (bad news = bad). Case: 2022–23 hot payroll mornings with red equity screens. *In a hiking cycle, markets grade the rate path.*

## Ch 16 · Trusting the Data, Trading the Gap
**16.1 Revisions & data quality**: data is an estimate. NFP revises the prior two months (tens of thousands of jobs); surveys are rarely revised; hard data is revised for months or years. **A headline beat with big downward revisions = a weaker trend** (yields can fall on an "in-line" report). Headline + revisions + trend = the real message. Recap: leading (surveys, claims, permits) vs coincident (payrolls, IP) vs lagging (unemployment rate, CPI).
**16.2 The expectations game (capstone)**: full chain: reality → release (lags, revisions) → surprise vs consensus → rate expectations → yields → FX, equities, gold → a new consensus. Only the *gap* moved prices, never whether a level was "high". **"Priced in" is a dial**: fresh (little priced, most upside) → spreading (partly priced) → fully priced and crowded (fuel spent; only sellers left). Conviction should fall as the dial turns. Case: 2024, ~6 Fed cuts priced in January; inflation stalled in Q1 → pricing unwound → 10y ~3.9% → ~4.7% by late April, USD up; the Fed eventually cut 3 times from September. *The repricing is the trade.*

## Practice questions (mine, course style)
1. Services PMI 55 → 53 → 51.5; manufacturing 48 → 49 → 50.5. How do you describe the economy, and which series matters more for the currency? → Services is still expanding but losing momentum; manufacturing is recovering. Services matters more.
2. NFP +210k vs +170k consensus, but prior months revised −90k and AHE +0.1% vs +0.3% expected. Net read? → Weaker than the headline suggests; dovish lean
3. Initial claims rise 5 weeks in a row from 220k to 265k before NFP. What should you expect? → Elevated risk of a weak payrolls print
4. CPI YoY previous 3.3%, consensus 3.1%, actual 3.2%. Classify. → Mild upside surprise
5. Markets price 4 cuts; retail sales +1.2% vs +0.3%. Expected moves in 2y, USD, S&P, gold, *if rates mode*. → Up, up, down, down
6. Where on the "priced-in dial" is a trade idea every newsletter is already recommending, with positioning at record extremes? → Fully priced and crowded; low conviction, reversal risk
7. Open response: explain why gold might fall on a PMI that *rises* from 44 to 47. → The improving rate of change lifts growth expectations → fewer cuts/higher real yields → gold's opportunity cost rises (or a risk-on rotation out of havens)
