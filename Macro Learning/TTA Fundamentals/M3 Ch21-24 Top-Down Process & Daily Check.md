# TTA · Ch 21–24 (Top-down thinking → Relative value & risk → Framework in practice → Scenarios & the daily habit)
Condensed notes in my own words. `[Claude+]` = my additions. **This is the course's end product: a repeatable process.**

## Ch 21 · Top-Down Thinking
**21.1 The nine-step funnel** (fixed order, slow → fast):
1. Growth: direction and acceleration
2. Inflation: direction (headline and core)
3. Central bank: what it wants to do given 1–2
4. Expectations: what consensus believes
5. Rates: the priced path, and *my gap* vs it
6. Relative value: same read on the other country
7. Asset impact: which assets move and which expresses it most purely
8. Positioning: is it crowded?
9. Risk: what proves me wrong, **written before entry**
The point of a process is that it makes you *checkable* (you can find which step broke). Order protects you: reading market pricing first makes you rationalise it; reading positioning first makes you a crowd-follower. The output is a probability-weighted view.
**21.2 Steps 1–3, reading the economy**: compress to one sentence: "growth is doing X, inflation Y, so the bank leans Z". Use direction and rate of change. No market prices at this stage. Can't write the sentence → get more data. Course replay of 2022: growth slowing + inflation ~9% + Fed hiking (incl. four 75bp moves) described the year.
**21.3 Steps 4–5, what's priced**: you're paid only for the **gap** between your read and pricing. Read pricing from rate futures and short-dated yields → meeting-by-meeting odds → implied path → **terminal rate** (where the path flattens). Your view = pricing → no trade even if right. Always ask: what if they deliver exactly what's priced / more / less?

## Ch 22 · Relative Value and Risk
**22.1 Steps 6–7**: run steps 1–5 for the other country; the pair favours the side whose expected rates have more upside *vs pricing*. Equal gaps → the pair shouldn't trend. Map to assets (FX, 2y bonds, equities, gold) and check they tell **one story**. Cross-asset disagreement is a warning (e.g. an FX view says hawkish repricing but 2y yields won't rise → find out why).
**22.2 Steps 8–9, positioning & kill switch**: crowded = less fuel, more unwind risk → lower conviction. Kill switch = 2–3 observable triggers tied to your own steps (data breaking the 1–2 read, CB language breaking 3, pricing closing the 5 gap). Written in advance (a smoke alarm installed before the fire). No falsifier = a belief, not a view. It also enables honest post-mortems (trigger fired = analysis broke; random shock = process may be fine).

## Ch 23 · The Framework in Practice
**23.1 One page**: the course = nine questions in order; each maps back to earlier chapters. Any "I don't know" in steps 1–5 → stop and gather information. **"No view" is a valid output**, and most days the checklist stops early.
**23.2–23.3 Worked example** (invented Canada scenario): growth rolling over, inflation falling toward target, BoC dropping hike language → dovish bias. Pricing: 2 cuts; the read argues for ~4; US side has no gap → the whole gap is Canadian → USDCAD higher and Canadian 2y yields lower are the purest expressions; equities "mixed" (rate relief vs weak growth). Key line of thinking: *the first two cuts are already priced; the view only earns on cuts three and four.* The trade is never "the bank will cut", but "the bank will cut more than priced".

## Ch 24 · Scenarios and the Daily Habit
**24.1 Where the market could be wrong**: turn the view into three scenarios with rough odds (base, dovish surprise, hawkish surprise), each with **one observable invalidation line**. When one fires, shift its weight to the survivors. Catalysts (CPI, jobs, CB meetings) are scheduled tests. The kill switch includes the *happy* exit: the market pricing your full view = the edge is gone. Case: 2024 (~6 cuts priced → hot Q1 CPI prints repeatedly fired the dovish-case kill line → 1–2 cuts priced by spring → the Fed delivered 3 from September).
**24.2 Your Turn**: the course gives a data-rich fictional UK scenario to run through all nine steps on paper, then compare with model answers (open-response questions). Differences in emphasis or expression are fine; the order, the "only unpriced views pay" logic and a written kill switch are non-negotiable.
**24.3 The Daily Check** (~15 min each morning; view maintenance, not reinvention; the nine steps are weekly/per-thesis research):
1. What moved overnight (USD, yields, oil, gold, indices), and what single story fits?
2. What's scheduled today, and what's the consensus?
3. What's priced for the central banks (next meeting odds, cuts/hikes over 12 months)?
4. What challenged expectations since yesterday (surprises, speakers, revisions)?
5. Where could today surprise, and which way would it reprice? **Write the if/then before the release.**
6. Are my biases still valid (supports still true, kill lines untouched)?

## Practice (mine, course style)
1. Your read: the ECB cuts 4 times this year. €STR futures price 4. What does step 5 say? → No gap, no edge
2. US read: hawkish gap of ~1 extra hike. Eurozone: dovish gap of ~1 extra cut. What do you expect for EURUSD vs a case where only the US has a gap? → A stronger downside case: both gaps point the same way
3. Your USD-bullish view is right on data, but US 2y yields keep falling. What does step 7 tell you to do? → Treat it as a warning; investigate before risking money
4. Write a three-scenario plan with invalidation lines for "gold higher over the next month" (open response). → Model: base (real yields drift lower, CB buying steady; killed if the 10y TIPS yield rises >30bp), bullish surprise (risk-off/fiscal stress; killed if VIX stays <15 and HY spreads tighten), bearish surprise (hot core CPI reprices the Fed; killed if core MoM ≤0.2)
5. Morning tape: USD down, US 2y down 8bp, gold +$25, SPX futures +0.6%. One story? → Rate expectations moved lower (dovish repricing)
6. You reach step 2 and inflation data is mixed (headline down, core up, services sticky). What does the checklist say? → Don't force it; gather more (3–6 month trend, services and wages) or accept "no view"
