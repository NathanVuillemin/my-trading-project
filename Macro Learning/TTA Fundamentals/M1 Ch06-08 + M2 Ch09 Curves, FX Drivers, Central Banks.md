# TTA · Ch 6–9 (Yield curve & real rates → FX → What drives a currency → Central banks)
Condensed notes in my own words. `[Claude+]` = my additions.

## Ch 6 · The Yield Curve and Real Rates
**6.1 Short vs long rates**: the curve plots yields by maturity. The **short end (2y)** tracks expected policy rates over the next ~2 years; the **long end (10y+)** reflects decade-long growth and inflation expectations plus a premium for tying money up. **2s10s = 10y − 2y** is the standard summary. Diagnostic habit: see *which end moved* to know which story changed (Fed hints move the 2y first; growth/inflation rethinks move the 10y).
**6.2 Shape changes**: steepening, flattening, inversion (short > long = cuts expected, usually because growth is seen weakening). Zones: front end (policy), belly (~5y), long end (growth/inflation). Four moves: bull steepener (front falls most → cuts coming), bear steepener (long rises most → inflation or supply worry), bull flattener (long falls most → flight to safety, growth fears), bear flattener (front rises most → hikes coming). Inversion has a strong recession record but **no fixed timing**. Case: 2022–23 US inversion (the deepest since the early 1980s) with no recession on the expected schedule. Expectations can be wrong or early.
**6.3 Nominal vs real**: real ≈ nominal − expected inflation; observable via TIPS. Positive real = savers gain purchasing power; negative = cash erodes. Rising inflation expectations with steady nominals → lower real rates → money hunts real assets like gold. Many pros treat the real rate as the **master variable**. Case: 2022, when the US 10y real yield swung from about −1% to about +1.5%; gold fell from ~$2,050 (Mar) to ~$1,620 (autumn) and long-duration tech slumped. *Judge gold against the real yield, not the headline rate.*

## Ch 7 · Reading the FX Market
**7.1 Pairs and pips**: base/quote; buying the pair = long base, short quote. Pip = 4th decimal (2nd for JPY pairs). Lots: standard 100k (~$10/pip on EURUSD), mini 10k (~$1), micro 1k (~$0.10). Majors all include USD. Always know pip value for your size before trading.
**7.2 Why currencies move**: four forces: **rates** (most frequent driver), **growth** (attracts investment), **flows** (capital flows ≫ trade flows), **mood** (fear → havens). Engine: data → expectations → flows → price (e.g. strong US data → higher expected US rates → dollar buying).

## Ch 8 · What Drives a Currency
**8.1 Rate differentials & carry**: pairs respond to the *gap* between two countries' rates, and especially to *changes* in the expected gap. Carry trade: borrow the low-yielder, hold the high-yielder, collect the difference, pushing the pair toward the high-yielder. The catch: a sudden move in the funding currency can erase years of carry, so carry thrives in calm markets and unwinds in fear. Case: 2022 EURUSD to below parity, with the Fed far ahead of the ECB (plus Europe's energy import bill). *The widening gap mattered, not the levels.*
**8.2 Safe havens & sentiment**: risk-on → AUD, NZD, EM up; risk-off → **USD, JPY, CHF** up (USD = reserve/deep markets; JPY = carry unwinds; CHF = stability). AUDJPY is a live fear gauge. Risk-off is cross-asset (stocks down, govt bonds up, havens up) but "usually, not always". Case: 2022 yen collapse (USDJPY ~115 → ~150) despite haven status, because the rate gap swamped it. **Dollar smile**: USD strong in global panic *and* when the US outgrows everyone; weakest when growth is fine everywhere and fear is low.
**8.3 FX is relative**: a pair is a seesaw of two economies. Compare **Growth, Inflation, Policy (GIP)** on both sides; the pair moves on the *differences*. A currency can rise on bad news if the other side is worse. The same currency can rise vs one and fall vs another on the same day.
**8.4 When a rate advantage fails**: a high rate is an offer someone must accept. Reasons it fails: already priced; inflation eats the yield (use *real* rates); panic hikes read as distress; political/institutional risk (you're lending into courts, CB independence, capital-exit rules; some funds can't go there at all); fiscal fright (yields up *and* currency down together, e.g. UK Sept 2022); global risk-off (havens win, carry unwinds); growth cracking (cuts priced early, currency softens while its rate is still top). Case: Turkey 2021+ (cuts under political pressure while inflation rose; lira ~8 → ~18/USD in 2021; inflation >80% in 2022). *Rates can change in a day; credibility takes years.* Takeaway: check who pays most, then check who wants to hold it.

## Ch 9 · How Central Banks Work (Module 2 starts)
**9.1 Why they exist**: the banks' bank; job = trustworthy money ≈ low, stable inflation (~2%). Mandates: the Fed has a **dual mandate** (prices + max employment); most others put inflation first. Tool: the overnight **policy rate**, which ripples into everything. Independence lets them act unpopularly. **Thermostat** analogy: hot → raise rates; cold → cut. Markets trade *where the thermostat is heading*, so data moves markets via the next decision.
**9.2 The Big Four**: Fed (USD; dual mandate; sets the global tone), ECB (EUR; one rate for ~20 economies; price stability first, cautious), BoE (GBP; 2% target; a public letter if the miss is >1pt), BoJ (JPY; decades fighting *too-low* inflation, so near-zero rates for very long). Meetings roughly every ~6 weeks with a statement and press conference. Rank banks by **direction of travel**: hiking/expected to hike supports the currency vs a cutter.
**9.3 Toolkit**: hike/cut/hold (usually 25bp steps; 1bp = 0.01%); balance sheet: **QE** (buy bonds with new money → yields down → push into risk assets) vs **QT** (drain). QE eras: stocks up, credit strong, low vol. QT/hiking eras remove the support ("liquidity backdrop"). **FX intervention** (e.g. Japan buying yen in Sept/Oct 2022 and again in 2024 past 160): violent short-term jolt but **buys time only** if the rate gap still points the other way.

> `[Claude+]` **Gold links**: 6.3 is the heart of your gold bias (see MMH 23). A practical rule: gold rallying while TIPS yields rise = a non-rate driver (CB buying, geopolitics) is in charge, so avoid fading it on real-yield logic alone. The dollar smile (8.2) also explains why gold can fall in the "US outperforming" phase even without a crisis.

> `[Claude+]` **Intervention in practice (USDJPY)**: watch for verbal-warning escalation from Japan's MoF ("excessive moves", "won't rule out any options"). Rate checks often come first. Moves of 3–5 big figures in minutes can happen, so don't hold tight-stop JPY longs into those zones.

## Practice questions (mine, course style)
1. 2y yield 4.8%, 10y 4.3%. Shape, and 2s10s? → Inverted, −0.5pt
2. The 2y falls 30bp, the 10y falls 5bp after weak retail sales. Name the move. → Bull steepener
3. Nominal 10y 4.2%, breakeven 2.5%. Real yield? What's the likely effect on gold if the real yield rises 50bp? → 1.7%; bearish gold, all else equal
4. Mini lot EURUSD bought at 1.0920, sold at 1.0985. P&L? → +65 pips × $1 = +$65
5. US and Japan rates unchanged, but markets start pricing BoJ hikes. Direction for USDJPY and why? → Lower; the expected gap narrows in the yen's favour
6. A central bank hikes 300bp in an emergency, and the currency keeps falling. Best explanation? → Read as distress/credibility failure; money still wants out
7. Which is the odd one out in a risk-off wave: CHF, JPY, NZD, USD? → NZD
8. The ECB is hiking while the Fed is on hold, yet EURUSD falls. Name two possible reasons. → Hikes already priced; energy/terms-of-trade outflows; risk-off favouring USD; eurozone growth cracking → cuts priced sooner
