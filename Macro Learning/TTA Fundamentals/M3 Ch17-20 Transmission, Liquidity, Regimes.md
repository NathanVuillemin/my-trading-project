# TTA · Ch 17–20 (Transmission chain → Credit & correlations → Liquidity & risk appetite → Positioning, regimes, govt debt)
Condensed notes in my own words. `[Claude+]` = my additions.

## Ch 17 · The Transmission Chain
**17.1 The map**: data surprise → expectations → repriced CB path → yields → FX, equities, gold, credit. Speed order: expectations (seconds) → yields (almost instantly) → the rest (hours to days). When something moves hard, ask *which link moved* and *what hasn't caught up yet*.
**17.2 Yields & the dollar**: FX follows the **rate differential** (e.g. US 2y − DE 2y), specifically its *unexpected change*. Buying foreign bonds requires buying the currency first. Conditional: in panics USD can rise while US yields fall, and a fully expected widening can produce no move.
**17.3 Real yields & gold**: gold's opportunity cost = the real yield on safe bonds (nominal − expected inflation). Real yields up → headwind; down → tailwind. It's a *headwind, not a guarantee*: fear demand and **central-bank buying** can override it. Case: 2023–24, with US 10y real yields near decade highs (~2%) while gold rose from ~$1,800 to >$2,700 as CBs bought >1,000t/yr (~double the prior decade). Two questions before any gold view: *what are real yields doing, and is a bigger price-insensitive buyer overriding them?*

## Ch 18 · Credit, Equities and Conditional Links
**18.1 Growth, credit, equities**: cyclicals (airlines, autos, banks, builders) vs defensives (food, healthcare, utilities). **Credit spread** = corporate − government yield = the price of default risk. Optimism: cyclicals lead, spreads tight. Worry: defensives lead, spreads widen. Spreads can widen well before defaults or equity weakness. When credit and equities disagree, **credit is usually the more honest witness**.
**18.2 Correlation ≠ law**: correlation describes a recent regime. Growth-fear regime: stocks and bonds move opposite (bonds hedge). Inflation-fear regime: they fall together. Beware third-factor drivers (rates, USD, liquidity). Case: 2022 (US equities ~−19%, Treasuries ~−13%, 60/40 ~−17%). *Diversification fails when inflation is the problem.*

## Ch 19 · Liquidity and Risk Appetite
**19.1 Liquidity's three meanings**: market (trade without moving price), central-bank (QE adds / QT drains), funding (ability to borrow against positions; margin hikes force sales *regardless of view*). They feed each other and dry up together; the riskiest assets feel it first. Case: March 2020, when Treasuries seized (10y ~0.5% → ~1.2% in days during a stock crash) until massive Fed buying. Judge liquidity before size.
**19.2 Risk-on / risk-off teams**:
| Arena | Risk-on | Risk-off |
|---|---|---|
| Equities | cyclicals, small caps | defensives (fall least) |
| Credit | high yield | govt bonds (usually) |
| FX | AUD, NZD, CAD, EM | USD, JPY, CHF |
| Commodities | copper, oil | gold |
Havens rely on trust and liquidity, and the status can fail (bonds in 2022). Daily first question: which mood?
**19.3 Fear gauges**: **VIX** (implied vol from SPX options): <15 calm, 15–25 normal/nervous, >30 stressed, 50+ crisis. Implied vs realised: implied far above realised = paying for fear; realised outrunning implied = the market was caught off guard. **HY spreads**: ~3% comfortable, ~6% worried, >8% serious trouble. **MOVE** (Treasury vol): <80 calm, >120 stressed, 150+ trouble. High MOVE = policy uncertainty spilling into FX and equities (in 2022 bonds panicked first). Financial conditions indices combine rates, spreads, USD, equities. **Read the change, not the level.** Case: 5 Aug 2024 VIX ~65 intraday, closed ~38, ~20 within a week. Fear moves faster than the economy.

## Ch 20 · Positioning, Regimes and Government Debt
**20.1 Crowded trades**: moves need *new* money; if everyone already owns it, good news can't lift it and a scare triggers a stampede ("pain trade"). Source: **COT report** (weekly large-spec net positioning); extremes flag crowding. Positioning gives **asymmetry, not direction**: a crowded long can grind higher but can't absorb surprises. Crowding + rising fear gauges = where violent unwinds start. Case: yen carry unwind 2024 (BoJ hike 31 Jul + weak US data → USDJPY ~162 early July → ~142 by 5 Aug; Nikkei ~−12% in a day).
**20.2 Regimes**: the dominant fear (growth vs inflation) sets reactions. Inflation regime: strong data → more hikes → bonds and stocks down together. Growth regime: strong data = relief. **Growth × inflation quadrants**: Goldilocks (G↑ I↓: stocks, risk FX); Reflation/overheating (G↑ I↑: commodities, cyclicals; bonds suffer); **Stagflation (G↓ I↑: gold, cash, USD; stocks and bonds struggle)**; Recession/disinflation (G↓ I↓: govt bonds, defensives). Detect the regime from *reactions* (stocks falling on strong data = inflation regime). Case: UK gilt crisis Sept 2022 (unfunded tax cuts with ~10% inflation → 30y gilt ~3.8% → >5%, GBPUSD record low ~1.03, LDI pension collateral calls → BoE intervention). *Flight to safety depends on the regime.*
**20.3 Government finances**: deficit = new bond supply that must be absorbed → a lower price / higher yield to attract buyers. Gauges: **debt/GDP** (many developed economies ~100%+; Japan ~double) and the **interest bill** (rising as cheap debt refinances at higher rates; e.g. 1% debt rolled at 4% costs ~3% more per year on that stock). Fiscal stress breaks the usual rule: **bonds and currency fall together** (capital leaving, not chasing yield). Slow pressure: ignored for years, repriced in weeks. Case: H2 2023 (bigger US auctions, the Fed on hold, 10y ~3.8% Jul → ~5% Oct, bear steepening). *Yields can rise on who is selling, not only on the CB.*

> `[Claude+]` **Stagflation corner = gold's best macro quadrant** in this course's map, and it matches the "credibility bid" in MMH 23. Your weekly bias could literally start with: growth direction (PMI trend, claims) × inflation direction (core CPI 3m annualised vs 6m) → quadrant → default bias for XAUUSD and USD.

> `[Claude+]` **Fiscal-stress tell for USD & gold**: if US long yields rise *while* DXY falls and gold rises (the 20.3 pattern), that's a credibility/fiscal regime. Don't treat higher yields as USD-bullish or gold-bearish in that case.

## Practice questions (mine, course style)
1. US 2y rises 15bp, German 2y rises 2bp after US CPI. Differential change and EURUSD direction? → Widens ~13bp in USD's favour; EURUSD down
2. Real yields rise 40bp over a month but gold rises 4%. Name two possible explanations. → Central-bank buying; fear/geopolitical demand; USD weakness from fiscal stress
3. HY spreads widen from 3.1% to 4.6% while the S&P sits near highs. What does the course say about trusting the equity rally? → Be sceptical; credit is usually the more honest witness
4. VIX goes from 13 to 29 in three sessions; realised vol is still low. Interpretation? → Fear is being priced quickly (protection bid); reduce size
5. COT shows record net-long positioning in gold futures. A mildly hawkish Fed statement lands. Why could gold drop more than the news deserves? → A crowded long unwinds; few new buyers
6. Strong NFP → S&P −1.2%, 10y +12bp, TLT down. Which regime? → Inflation-fear regime
7. A government announces unfunded spending; 30y yields +40bp and the currency −3%. Why aren't higher yields supporting the currency? → Fiscal credibility fear: capital leaving, not seeking yield
8. Quadrant: growth falling, inflation rising. Which assets does the course say usually hold up? → Gold, cash, USD
