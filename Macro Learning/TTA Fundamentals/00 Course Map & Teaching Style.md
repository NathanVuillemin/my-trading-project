# The Trading Academy — Fundamentals: course map & teaching style
Private study notes by Claude for Nathan (paid course; notes are condensed and paraphrased, never copied). `[Claude+]` = my additions.

## Structure
3 modules → 24 chapters → 68 lessons, a module test after each module, and a final exam. Other app sections: Daily Check (6 questions, ~15 min each morning), Review, Glossary, flashcards, and a mistakes log.
- **Module 1, Understand the Machine**: 1 Inside a Market (4) · 2 Positions & Price Moves (3) · 3 Mapping the Markets (3) · 4 Currencies, Commodities & Contracts (3) · 5 Interest & Bond Pricing (3) · 6 Yield Curve & Real Rates (3) · 7 Reading the FX Market (2) · 8 What Drives a Currency (4) → *Module 1 Test: Market Mechanics*
- **Module 2, Understand What Moves Markets**: 9 How Central Banks Work (3) · 10 Words, Surprises & Liquidity (3) · 11 What Inflation Is & How It's Measured (3) · 12 Inflation Expectations & Markets (2) · 13 Growth & the Business Cycle (3) · 14 Jobs & the Rate of Change (2) · 15 Reading the Data (3) · 16 Trusting the Data, Trading the Gap (2) → *Module 2 Test: The Macro Engine*
- **Module 3, Think Like a Macro Trader**: 17 The Transmission Chain (3) · 18 Credit, Equities & Conditional Links (2) · 19 Liquidity & Risk Appetite (3) · 20 Positioning, Regimes & Government Debt (3) · 21 Top-Down Thinking (3) · 22 Relative Value & Risk (2) · 23 The Framework in Practice (3) · 24 Scenarios & the Daily Habit (3) → *Final Exam: Reasoning About Markets*

## How every lesson is built (the template)
1. **Title + one-line hook**
2. **The Idea**: the concept in plain words, often with an everyday analogy (auction house, rope tug-of-war, rent for money)
3. **Why It Matters**: why a trader should care
4. **How It Works**: the mechanism, often a small table or an arrow chain (A → B → C → D)
5. **A Simple Example**: concrete numbers (pips, $ per contract, coupon ÷ price)
6. **Market Connection**: links to other markets and forward references to later chapters
7. **What Actually Happened** (in many lessons): a real episode written as *event → mechanism → lesson*
8. **Trader Takeaway**: one actionable rule
9. **Check Your Understanding**: 3–4 multiple-choice questions, 4 options each

## The course's through-line
Prices move when **beliefs change**, and beliefs change on **new information vs what was expected**. Three questions organise everything:
1. What does the market currently expect?
2. Is that expectation wrong?
3. If it shifts, what reprices, and by how much?
Every later tool (yields, rate futures, inflation/growth/jobs data, cross-asset links) serves one of these three.

## How the quizzes test (so I can mimic them)
- **Definition checks**: "which player earns the bid/ask gap?"; "what does the face value represent?"
- **Mechanical calculations**: pips × $/pip, contract size × price move, coupon ÷ price, spread = 10y − 2y, leverage % of account.
- **Execution mechanics**: "you buy at market; which price do you pay?" (the ask).
- **Scenario → best explanation**: a market reaction is described and you pick the *best explanation*. Distractors are typical beginner errors ("rate hikes always strengthen a currency", "the market misread it", conspiracy options).
- **Signal reading**: e.g. a commodity falling alongside weak surveys → which inference is sound.
- **Classification**: name the curve shape or move from given yields; liquid vs volatile vs illiquid.
- Distractor patterns: sign flips (gain vs loss), off-by-10 magnitudes ($1 / $100 / $1,000), "nothing happens because X is fixed", and absolute/over-strong claims.
- The spaced-repetition features (Daily Check, Review, mistakes log) mean the course expects **retrieval practice**, so quizzing you in the same style fits.
