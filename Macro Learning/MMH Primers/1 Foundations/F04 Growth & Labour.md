# F4 · Growth & Labour
Big idea: most macro debates are really about **where we are in the business cycle** relative to potential output.

## Growth
- **GDP** = value of everything produced. Headline "growth" = **real** GDP (inflation removed).
- **Cycle**: expansion → peak → contraction → trough → recovery, oscillating around **potential GDP** (sustainable trend, estimated only).
- **Output gap** = actual − potential. Positive → overheating and inflation pressure. Negative → slack and disinflation. Monetary policy is essentially output-gap management.
- **Recession**: rule of thumb is 2 negative quarters of real GDP. The official US call (NBER) is broader and comes late, long after markets have moved.
- **Landings**: soft (inflation down, no recession), hard (hikes break something), "no landing" (growth doesn't slow and inflation risk returns).
- **Productivity** = output per hour. The only way to get lasting growth and real wage gains without inflation.
- **PMI / ISM**: surveys where 50 is the dividing line. Early and rarely revised, which is why markets love them.

## Labour (where growth meets inflation)
- **Unemployment rate**: lagging. Rises decisively only once a recession is underway; small rises from lows matter.
- **NFP**: monthly net change in payrolls. Roughly +150–200k is steady state. Heavily revised → watch the 3-month average.
- **Initial jobless claims**: weekly (Thursday), the fastest labour data. Sustained rises lead payrolls.
- **Participation rate**: the denominator trap. Unemployment can fall because people give up looking.
- **Wage growth (AHE)**: ~3% is compatible with 2% inflation; 5%+ usually isn't. The Fed reads it before the headline.
- **JOLTS**: openings per unemployed = the tightness gauge. Openings falling without layoffs is the soft-landing path.

## Self-test
1. The output gap is +2%. What does the central bank likely worry about? → Inflation / overheating
2. Why do pros prefer the 3-month average of NFP? → A single print is noisy and heavily revised
3. Unemployment falls from 4.0% to 3.8% while participation drops. Good or bad? → Possibly bad: people leaving the labour force
4. Which labour series gives the earliest warning? → Initial jobless claims
5. ISM prints 47. What does it say? → Manufacturing is contracting
