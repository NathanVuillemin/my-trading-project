# F7 · Risk & Global Macro
Big idea: two moods (risk-on / risk-off) explain more of a day than any headline. The balance-of-payments mirror is the spine of the course.

## Risk-on vs risk-off
| Risk-on buys | Risk-off buys |
|---|---|
| Equities, HY credit, AUD/CAD, EM, copper, crypto | Govt bonds, USD, JPY, CHF, gold |
- **The tell**: not one asset falling, but *all* risk assets falling together while havens rally.
- **VIX** (30-day S&P implied vol): mid-teens calm, 20s nervous, 30+ stressed, 50+ crisis.
- Haven status is habit + liquidity + trust, and it can change: bonds did **not** hedge in 2022.
- **Flight to quality**: everyone de-risks at once through narrow exits → fast moves.
- **Sentiment** is useful as a contrarian signal at extremes.

## Global macro vocabulary
- Cycles usually end through policy tightening or a shock, not old age.
- **Monetary** (central bank) vs **fiscal** (government). The *mix* matters; the 2020s = big fiscal + tight monetary.
- **Trade balance** (goods and services) vs **current account** (adds income flows).
- **Capital flows** mirror the current account exactly: a CA deficit = a capital inflow. Module 00 is built on this.
- **Global liquidity**: central bank balance sheets + credit + offshore dollars. A rising tide lifts most assets; when it ebbs, leverage sinks first (Bitcoin is the extreme case).

## Self-test
1. Stocks −2%, USDJPY −1.5%, gold +1%, AUDUSD −1%. What regime is this? → Risk-off
2. VIX at 32. How would you describe the market? → Stressed
3. A country runs a large CA deficit. What must be true of its capital account? → Equal net capital inflow
4. Why do risk-off moves tend to be faster than risk-on moves? → Everyone de-risks at once; few havens → crowded exits
5. Name the policy mix that defined the 2020s. → Loose fiscal + tight monetary
