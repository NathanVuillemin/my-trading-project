# F5 · Credit & FX
Big idea: credit spreads are the **early-warning light**, and FX is always **relative** (one currency up = another down).

## Credit
- **Credit spread** = corporate yield − government yield (same maturity) = the price of default risk. Widening = stress, often before equities react.
- **IG vs HY**: BBB- and above is investment grade. Below that is high yield / junk, with wide spreads and equity-like behaviour in crises.
- **Ratings** (AAA → D): downgrades to junk force selling by IG-only funds.
- **Default rate** confirms trouble late; **spreads** anticipate it.
- **Financial conditions index**: rates + spreads + USD + equities combined. Conditions can *ease* while the Fed hikes.
- **Liquidity** has two meanings: market (ease of trading size) and macro (amount of money and funding in the system). Both vanish at the worst time.

## FX
- A pair's price = units of quote per 1 base. EURUSD ↑ = EUR stronger / USD weaker. **Rising pair = base strengthening.**
- Drivers: **rate differentials** (dominant month to month), growth/inflation and capital flows (over years), risk sentiment (can override everything for a week).
- **Reserve currency**: USD. Cheaper US borrowing, and the world is exposed to the Fed through dollar debt.
- **DXY**: USD vs a basket, heavily euro-weighted. DXY ↑ tightens global conditions.
- **Carry trade**: borrow the low-yielder, hold the high-yielder. Steady gains, then violent unwinds when risk turns ("up the stairs, down the elevator").

## Self-test
1. The HY spread goes from 350bp to 550bp while stocks are flat. How do you read that? → Credit is flagging stress ahead of equities
2. USDJPY rises. Which currency strengthened? → USD (the base)
3. US rates 5%, Japan 0.5%. What's the carry trade, and what kills it? → Long USD / short JPY; a risk-off shock → unwind
4. Why does a strong DXY hurt emerging markets? → Their dollar debt gets more expensive to service
5. Can financial conditions ease during a hiking cycle? How? → Yes, if equities rally and spreads tighten
