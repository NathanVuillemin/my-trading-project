# F3 · Inflation & Central Banks
Big idea: inflation is a *rate of change*, and central banks react to **surprises in their stance**, not to their stance itself.

## Inflation family
- Inflation = % change in the general price level over a year.
- **Disinflation** (rate falls but stays positive → prices still rising) ≠ **deflation** (rate below zero → prices falling). Central banks fear deflation more.
- **CPI vs core CPI**: core removes food and energy because they're noisy. Core shows the trend; headline shows what households feel.
- **PCE / core PCE**: broader basket with weights that adjust as people substitute. Core PCE is the gauge behind the Fed's 2% target. Usually runs a bit below CPI.
- **PPI**: producer prices; an early pipeline signal for consumer prices, and it feeds parts of PCE.
- **Breakevens**: market-implied inflation (nominal − TIPS yield); backed by money and updated live.
- **Expectations**: they're self-fulfilling (wage demands, pre-emptive price hikes), so "anchored" expectations are the central bank's goal.
- **Stagflation**: high inflation and weak growth at once. Traps the central bank (the 1970s).

## Central banks
- The big four are the Fed, ECB, BoE and BoJ. The Fed's dual mandate is 2% inflation and maximum employment.
- The main lever is the overnight **policy rate**, which anchors the short end of the whole curve.
- **Hawk ↔ dove axis**: markets price the *shift* along the axis. A hawkish surprise → yields ↑, risk assets wobble.
- **QE**: create reserves and buy bonds → yields ↓, investors pushed into riskier assets. Used near the zero bound.
- **QT**: let bonds roll off or sell them → reserves drain. Quieter than QE, but a steady withdrawal of liquidity.
- **Forward guidance**: talking about the future path. A credible sentence can move the 10y like a real cut.
- **Balance sheet**: bonds (assets) against reserves and currency (liabilities). A rough gauge of how much support the system is getting.
- **Meeting cycle**: FOMC meets 8x a year (decision → statement → press conference → minutes about 3 weeks later). The reaction is to the surprise.
- **Lender of last resort**: lends freely against good collateral in a panic. Having that backstop is what prevents most runs.

## Self-test
1. CPI goes from 5% to 3%. Are prices falling? → No; that's disinflation
2. Which number does the Fed's 2% target refer to? → Core PCE
3. The Fed holds rates but the statement is much more hawkish than priced. Expected reaction in yields, USD and stocks? → Yields ↑, USD ↑, stocks ↓ (usually)
4. Why can forward guidance move long yields without any rate change? → Markets price the whole expected path
5. Why is stagflation hard for a central bank? → Hiking fights inflation but deepens the slowdown, and vice versa
