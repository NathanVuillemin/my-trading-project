# F10 · Money & Banks
Big idea: **banks create money by lending**; repayment destroys it. Liquidity is measurable: reserves + bank credit + collateral capacity.

## Two-tier money
- **Reserves** (central bank money that banks settle with; cash is paper reserves) sit above **deposits** (what people spend), which sit above **credit** (loans, bonds).
- In a crisis everyone scrambles *up* the pyramid toward safer money = funding stress.

## Loans create deposits
- A $500k mortgage adds a $500k loan (asset) and a $500k deposit (liability) to the bank's balance sheet. New money is created in one entry.
- So the credit cycle **is** the money-supply cycle → credit conditions (F5) matter.

## Government side
- Deficit = annual spending − tax. Debt = accumulated deficits held as bonds (someone else's safe asset).
- **Treasuries** are the base asset: repo collateral, the risk-free leg of spreads, the panic destination.
- Issued at scheduled auctions to **primary dealers** (obliged to bid); auction results are a live signal.

## Self-test
1. Where does most money in the economy come from? → Commercial bank lending
2. What happens to the money supply when a loan is repaid? → It shrinks
3. Define macro liquidity in three parts. → CB reserves + bank credit creation + collateral/borrowing capacity
4. Why is a weak Treasury auction market-moving? → It reveals soft demand for the base asset → yields reprice
