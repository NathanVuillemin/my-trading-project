# F2 · Rates & Bonds
Big idea: a bond is a tradeable loan with **fixed** cash flows, so price and yield move in opposite directions.

## Mechanics
- Face value (repaid at maturity), coupon (fixed interest), maturity (end date).
- **Yield** = annual return if you buy at today's price and hold to maturity. Pay less for the same coupons → higher yield.
- **Yield curve** = government yields plotted from 1m to 30y.
  - Normal (upward): long lending pays more, growth expected
  - Flat: transition
  - Inverted: short > long, cuts expected → classic recession warning

## The four curve moves
Bull/bear = direction of yields (bull = yields ↓). Steepener/flattener = which end moved more.
| Move | What happens | Typical story |
|---|---|---|
| Bull steepener | Short end falls most | Fed cutting |
| Bull flattener | Long end falls most | Flight to safety |
| Bear steepener | Long end rises most | Supply / term premium |
| Bear flattener | Short end rises most | Fed hiking |

## Key terms
- **Duration**: price sensitivity to yields. Duration 7 ≈ −7% price for +1% yield. Longer maturity and lower coupon → higher duration.
- **Real yield**: nominal minus expected inflation; observable via TIPS. Acts as "gravity" for risk assets (and gold).
- **Breakeven inflation** = nominal − real yield at the same maturity → the market's live inflation forecast.
- **Term premium**: extra yield for lending long instead of rolling short. Estimated, not observed; when it rises the long end sells off regardless of the Fed.
- **Treasury auction**: how governments borrow. A weak auction (yield above expected) signals soft demand and moves the curve fast.
- **2s10s** = 10y − 2y. The standard one-number summary of the curve; negative = inverted.

## Self-test
1. A bond's price falls from $1,000 to $950. What happens to its yield, and why? → Rises; same fixed coupons for a lower price
2. 2y yield +30bp, 10y yield +10bp. Name the move and the likely driver. → Bear flattener; Fed hiking / hawkish repricing
3. 10y nominal 4.3%, 10y TIPS 2.0%. What's the breakeven? → 2.3%
4. A bond has duration 6 and yields rise 0.5%. Roughly what happens to its price? → About −3%
5. Long yields jump after a weak auction while Fed pricing is unchanged. Which component moved? → Term premium
