# F6 · Equities & Commodities
Big idea: **Price = EPS × P/E**. Macro mostly works through the multiple. Commodities are pure supply/demand with storage in between.

## Equities
- An index is usually market-cap weighted, so a few mega-caps can carry it (market cap = price × shares).
- EPS reactions depend on results *vs expectations*.
- **P/E**: years of current earnings you're paying for. High P/E = high expectations and high rate sensitivity.
- **Multiple expansion/compression**: 2022 was mostly compression (earnings held, rates crushed P/E). Always ask *which engine moved*.
- **Breadth**: narrow rallies are fragile. Gauges: % of stocks above the 200-day average, advancers vs decliners.
- **Growth vs value**: growth behaves like long duration (hurt by rising rates); value (banks, energy) often likes higher rates.
- **Cyclical vs defensive**: their relative performance is a daily vote on growth.
- **Total return** = price + dividends. Dividends are a big share over the long run.

## Commodities
- **Oil**: demand ← global growth; supply ← OPEC+, shale, geopolitics; inventories buffer. Supply adjusts slowly → big price swings. Oil also feeds CPI.
- **Gold**: yields nothing, so its cost is the forgone real yield → likes **falling real yields**, crises, and loss of trust in money or institutions. The "anti-confidence" asset.
- **Industrial vs precious**: copper = growth trade ("Dr. Copper"); gold = monetary/fear trade. Silver sits in both camps, hence its volatility.
- **Supercycle**: supply takes years to build → boom → overbuild → bust → underinvestment → next shortage.
- **Contango** (futures > spot: normal, storage + financing) vs **backwardation** (spot > futures: scarcity now).

## Self-test
1. The S&P is flat on the year but EPS grew 10%. What happened to the P/E? → Compressed ~9%
2. Real yields jump 50bp. First-order effect on gold and on growth stocks? → Both pressured
3. The oil curve flips from contango to backwardation. What does that signal? → Near-term physical tightness
4. Why is silver more volatile than gold? → Part industrial, part monetary: two sets of drivers
5. The index is up but only 30% of stocks are above their 200-day average. What's the concern? → Narrow breadth = fragile rally
