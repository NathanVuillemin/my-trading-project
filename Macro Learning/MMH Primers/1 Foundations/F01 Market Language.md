# F1 · Market Language
Source: Market Macro Hub primers, Foundations F1 (my study notes, paraphrased)
Big idea: prices move on the **gap between outcome and expectation**, not on the event itself.

## Everyday vocabulary
| Term | In one line |
|---|---|
| Basis point (bp) | 0.01 percentage point. 25bp = 0.25%. Rates are quoted in bp because moves are small. |
| Spread | Difference between two numbers, usually two yields (e.g. 2s10s, credit spread). "Widening" = gap grows. |
| Rally / sell-off | Sustained move up / down in *price*. Bond rally = prices up = **yields down**. |
| Long / short | Long profits if it rises; short (borrowed and sold) profits if it falls. |
| Bid / ask | Best price buyers pay / sellers accept. The gap is the cost of trading instantly; widens under stress. |
| Leverage | Borrowed exposure. 10x turns 1% into 10%. Creates forced sellers. |
| Volatility | Size of swings (annualised %). Not the same as direction. |
| Drawdown | Peak-to-trough fall. −20% needs +25% to recover (see 00.6). |
| Positioning / crowded trade | What the market already holds. Crowded = few buyers left, many potential forced sellers (see 00.4). |

## Expectations vocabulary (the logic of data → price)
- **Priced in**: already in the price. An expected Fed cut that happens moves nothing.
- **Consensus**: average economist forecast before a release; the benchmark for beat/miss/in-line.
- **Surprise index** (e.g. Citi): running score of beats vs misses. Mean-reverts because forecasters adapt.
- **Leading / coincident / lagging**: before the turn (permits, new orders, yield curve) / with it (payrolls, industrial production) / after it (unemployment rate, CPI).
- **Risk premium**: extra expected return for bearing risk. It exists *because* the bad outcome sometimes happens.
- **Correlation**: −1 to +1. Depends on the regime and tends to break when you rely on it (Module 08).
- **Nominal vs real**: real = after inflation. Markets care about real.
- **Absolute vs relative**: did it rise vs did it beat the alternative. Most pro mandates are relative.
- **Probability, not certainty**: a good trade has good odds even if it loses. Think in distributions.
- **YoY / MoM / annualised MoM**: slow and smooth / fast and noisy / "this month's pace for a year".
- **Base effects**: YoY can move because an extreme month *drops out* of the window.
- **Seasonally adjusted**: calendar patterns removed; big SA vs non-SA gaps become a story themselves.

## Habit to build
For every headline: *"Was that expected, or a surprise?"* → leads into 00.7 Pricing Data.

## Self-test
1. The 10y goes from 4.20% to 4.27%. How many bp? → 7bp
2. "Bonds rallied." What did yields do? → Fell
3. The Fed cuts 25bp exactly as expected. Why might USD barely move? → Already priced in; no surprise
4. Name one leading, one coincident and one lagging indicator. → e.g. building permits / payrolls / unemployment rate
5. Inflation YoY drops from 6% to 4% but MoM is unchanged. What's a likely explanation? → Base effect: a big month left the 12-month window
6. You lose 20%. What gain do you need to get back? → 25%
