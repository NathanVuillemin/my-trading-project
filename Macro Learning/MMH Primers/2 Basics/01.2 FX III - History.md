# 01.2 · FX III — What history teaches (Part 3 of 5)
Condensed summary in my own words. `[Claude+]` = my additions.

## Main lessons
- **Read history probabilistically**: hindsight makes outcomes look inevitable. Study each episode twice: once for what happened, and once for *what the odds looked like to people living it*.
- **Why study it**: it stands in for experience you haven't had, explains *why* data behaved as it did (the same rate spread means something different under a peg vs a float), and feeds scenario planning.
- **Continuity vs discontinuity**: there are always similarities and differences. Use base rates for the continuous causes and first-principles reasoning for the genuinely new parts. The classic error is using the wrong tool on the wrong half.
- **Why FX backtests mislead**: interventions (e.g. Japan 2022) are invisible in price history; old markets had fewer instruments and less transparency; some countries' data is manipulated (but accounting identities still hold). For anything idiosyncratic, go to **primary sources**, not later summaries.
- **Two study tracks**: the full history of the pair you trade + a library of major FX crises.
- **Recurring plot**: a fixed arrangement (paper promise, gold parity, peg, cap) collides with the Impossible Trinity, and the arrangement loses.
- **Tools**: crisis databases (International Crisis Behavior project), geopolitical/conflict trackers. Big balance-sheet shifts tend to follow geopolitical ruptures.

## Crisis library (dates to know)
| Era | Events |
|---|---|
| Paper & default | Mississippi Bubble 1719–20; South Sea 1720; 19th-c. Latin American defaults |
| Gold unravels | German hyperinflation 1921–23; UK off gold 1931; US devalues vs gold 1933–34; France/Belgium 1936; late-1940s devaluations; Nixon ends Bretton Woods 1971 |
| Pegs break | Black Wednesday (GBP out of ERM) 1992; Mexico 1994–95; Asia (THB first) 1997–98; Russia 1998; Brazil 1999; Argentina 2001–02 |
| Modern | Iceland 2008; SNB removes EURCHF floor 2015; TRY 2018; COVID USD spike then slide 2020 |

> `[Claude+]` **Gold-relevant history to add to your library**: 1971–80 gold bull ($35 → $850) after Bretton Woods ended plus 1970s inflation; the Volcker shock 1980–82 (real rates surge, gold halves); 1999 Washington Agreement on central bank gold sales; the 2011 peak (~$1,920) after QE and the eurozone crisis; the 2013 taper tantrum (gold −28% for the year); 2022–24 record central-bank buying after Russian reserves were frozen, with gold decoupling from US real yields.

> `[Claude+]` **SNB 2015 lesson for prop trading**: EURCHF fell ~30% in minutes and many brokers went bust. Pegs and caps create tail risk that stop-losses can't protect against; avoid holding size in pegged/capped pairs.
