# 01.1 · FX II — Synthesis (Part 2 of 5)
Condensed notes in my own words. `[Claude+]` = my additions.

## Four ground rules
1. Balance sheets and the BoP must balance (bookkeeping, not theory).
2. "Hold all else equal" is a teaching device; in reality everything moves at once.
3. Every model has an intended range; don't push it beyond that.
4. Every flow has a source and a use; flows net out, but under irreducible uncertainty.

## Framework
- **Currency ≈ equity in the country**: value the whole structure; don't extrapolate from one hot sector.
- **Chain**: relative GIP → returns and risk premia on each country's assets → transmitted through the **BoP** (current + capital/financial account) → FX spot and forwards. The economy producing the returns keeps changing, so redo the analysis as the structure shifts.
- **Core claim** (from a well-known FX textbook by an options trader): changes in the **risk premium** are the main driver of FX, ahead of rate differentials, BoP, inflation etc. The real question is what drives the premia (→ relative GIP). A currency's premium is linked to its home bond and equity premia.
- **CIP** (covered interest parity): forward = spot adjusted for the interest differential (no-arbitrage).
- **UIP** (uncovered parity): claims the forward = expected future spot. It usually fails; the gap is the **risk premium**, which is what carry harvests. Carry also acts as a cushion against adverse spot moves (separate from expected profit).
- Free real-rate proxy: TIP/WIP ETF ratio vs DXY; CME FX swap rate monitor for forwards.
- **Quantify three things together**: GIP differentials, BoP, Impossible Trinity. Blanket narratives ("spending weakens the currency") fail because nobody sizes the flow. Use NIPA and GDP/GDI line items.
- Current account (goods/services) ≈ mirror of the financial account (asset flows).
- **Path dependency**: current conditions constrain the plausible next steps.
- **Practice**: assess risk/reward on several horizons using level, speed and acceleration. Build an **extrapolation grid** (hold today's rate of change and ½x, 1x, 2x scenarios for 6 months) and **fade pricing that no credible path reaches**.
- **Regressions reflect the machine; they don't replace it**: correlations are time-bound. When they disagree with your framework, dig deeper instead of bolting on patches.

> `[Claude+]` **CIP formula**: F = S × (1 + i_quote·t) / (1 + i_base·t). Example: EURUSD spot 1.1000, USD 1y rate 4.5%, EUR 1y 2.5% → F ≈ 1.1000 × 1.045/1.025 ≈ 1.1215. The forward is *higher* because the base (EUR) has the lower rate, so forward points are positive. UIP would say EURUSD is expected to rise to 1.1215; historically high-yielders tend *not* to depreciate that much (the "forward premium puzzle"), which is why carry pays on average.

> `[Claude+]` **Gold carry analogy**: gold's "carry" is negative (no yield, plus storage), so its forward price sits above spot by roughly the USD rate minus the gold lease rate. That's the same CIP logic and explains why gold competes with real yields.
