# 00.5 · Building Your System
Summary in my own words. `[Claude+]` = my additions.

## Key ideas
- **Treat trading as a business.** The goal is P&L, not being right.
- **Two planning levels**: (1) the *Grand Plan* (operations, worst-case contingencies, structure/SOPs, capital allocation, risk strategy); (2) *each system* (beliefs → edge → filters → entries → stops → exits → statistics → **breakpoint**, the pre-agreed condition to stop trading it). Writing both down exposes gaps.
- Run **2–3 uncorrelated strategies** (e.g. trend + mean reversion).
- **Your trade statistics are the mirror**: win rate, average win/loss, streaks, split by setup/market/time. Systems stop you lying to yourself and reveal leakage (skipped trades, poor exits).
- **Mechanical vs rules-based discretionary**: both must answer "why did I take this trade?" Machines are consistent but unimaginative; humans are creative but sloppy. The *centaur* model automates the routine and saves your mental capital for judgement. Algorithms are cheap employees.
- **Design principles**: ideas first (no data mining); there are only a few trade archetypes; **low win rates are fine** with positive skew (edge often lives in exits, since everyone obsesses over entries); optimise **return vs drawdown**, not Sharpe. Smooth high-Sharpe profiles like option selling hide blow-up risk.
- **Bridge to sizing**: ideas don't make money; allocation and risk do. The same system with a different sizing method gives a different result. Low-vol instruments invite over-leverage.
- Expectancy = (win% × avg win) − (loss% × avg loss).

> `[Claude+]` **Your breakpoint, prop-firm style**: define it before starting, e.g. "stop and review if 8 consecutive losers, or live expectancy < 50% of backtest after 40 trades, or drawdown > 1.5× max backtest DD." For a 2R-average-winner system with 40% win rate: expectancy = 0.4×2 − 0.6×1 = **+0.2R per trade**.

> `[Claude+]` **Streak math**: with a 40% win rate, P(≥8 losses in a row somewhere in 200 trades) is high (roughly 70%+). Plan for it rather than being surprised by it.
