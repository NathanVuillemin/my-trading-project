# 00 · The Framework — the five-layer map
Source: MMH Basics 00 (study notes, my own words). `[Claude+]` = my additions, not from the course.

## Core thesis
Prices are the footprint of **capital moving across borders and sectors**. If you can explain where money flows and why, you can explain prices. The course's anchor quote (Paul Tudor Jones): the world is a flow chart for capital.
Pros watch **connections** between markets, not isolated tickers. Example: a hot US jobs print can move JGBs; a Chilean drought can move CLP through copper.

## The five layers (slow → fast)
| # | Layer | Timescale | Question it answers | Deep dive |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Country | Decades | Who is this economy structurally? | 00.1 |
| 2 | Economic data | Quarters/months | What is it doing right now? | 00.2 |
| 3 | Markets & fundamentals | Months/weeks | How does the data map to prices? | 00.3 |
| 4 | Expectations vs reality | Weeks/days | What's the surprise versus what's priced? | 00.7 |
| 5 | Positioning & plumbing | Days/hours | Who is already in the trade, and can it be financed? | 00.4 |
Structure at the top sets the stage; positioning at the bottom decides **timing**. The best trades line up several layers at once.

### Layer 1 — Country
Seven-part profile: history, demographics, output capacity, geography, resources/tech, politics/geopolitics, and the three regimes (currency, monetary, fiscal).
**Key discipline**: turn stories into numbers. Not "Chile has copper" but "copper = X% of GDP/exports" → that tells you how leveraged the economy is to the copper cycle.

### Layer 2 — Data: flows vs stocks
- **Flows** = activity per period (income statement). **Stocks** = accumulated holdings/debts (balance sheet).
- GDP (expenditure) = **C + I + G + (X − M)**. Rough US shares: C ≈ 68%, I ≈ 18%, G ≈ 17%, NX ≈ −3%. The consumer is two-thirds of the economy, hence the market's obsession with jobs and wages.
- Around it: income side (profits, wages), labour, sectors (services, retail, industry, housing, government), surveys (lead the hard data), trade/BoP, prices.
- Four sector balance sheets: households, companies, banks, government. **Every liability is someone else's asset**; markets are where those IOUs get priced.
- Habit: learn *why* a number moved, and watch every frequency (quarterly for the picture, monthly for the trend, weekly for the pulse).

### Layer 3 — Connect data to markets
Your economic and market views must be **one continuous story**; if they don't connect, one of them is wrong.
| Market | Natural anchor |
|---|---|
| Equities | Earnings/valuation ← consumption, wages, profits |
| Bonds/rates | Inflation, growth, central bank policy, credit risk |
| Commodities | Physical supply/demand, inventories, weather, geopolitics |
| FX | Cross-border flows, trade balance, investment, reserves |
- Example: a copper-heavy economy can be "watched" through listed miners' quarterly revenues, costs and debt.
- **Post-2022 transmission chain**: tight labour → wage gains → services inflation (services ≈ mostly wages) → Fed hikes → bond prices fall. Run it backwards too: how much must which industries shrink to loosen labour enough for bonds to rally?

### Layer 4 — Expectations vs reality
- Only the **surprise** is new information. Example: NFP +250k vs +200k expected → hotter than priced → rate expectations ↑, bonds ↓, USD ↑, stocks ambiguous (growth good vs rates bad).
- Tools: economic surprise indices (historically track equity swings), MoM direction of travel, earnings surprises.
- Layers 1–3 tell you which way surprises are *more likely* to land.
- **Policy creates divergences**: always monitor the central bank and the STIR market alongside the data.

#### Policy-regime quadrant (axes: real yields and inflation expectations)
| | Real yields falling | Real yields rising |
|---|---|---|
| **Inflation expectations rising** | **Easy money / QE**: gold and copper strong, stocks grind up, USD soft, risk FX up, credit happy | **Healthy expansion**: banks/energy/value lead, yields up, commodity FX outperforms, credit fine |
| **Inflation expectations falling** | **Recession**: govt bonds star, defensives, havens bid, oil/copper sold | **Tightening / QT**: cash king, USD strong, stocks and commodities struggle, buy protection, vol bid |
Real yield = nominal yield − expected inflation. Identify the quadrant → know which assets the tide favours.

> `[Claude+]` For gold specifically: the left column (falling real yields) is the tailwind. Gold in the top-right is usually mixed; bottom-right (QT, strong USD, rising real yields) is its hardest quadrant. Note that 2022–24 gold held up despite high real yields thanks to central-bank buying, so treat the map as a default, not a law.

### Layer 5 — Positioning & plumbing
- Layers 1–4 = what *should* happen. Positioning = **who has already bet on it**. The most violent moves come from crowded trades unwinding.
- Track: CFTC COT (weekly futures positioning), options/dealer hedging, fund flows (ETF, mutual, foreign buying), active vs passive vs systematic (forced, price-insensitive flow).
- **Plumbing = repo**: overnight cash loans against bond collateral. Cash-rich (MMFs, corporates) → dealers → cash-hungry (hedge funds). The Fed sets the floor/ceiling for overnight rates. Clogged repo = every market feels it.
- **Microstructure**: overnight futures sessions, seasonality, order imbalances, available liquidity. Thin liquidity → small flows, big moves.

## Takeaways
1. Every price is downstream of capital flows.
2. Quantify country narratives.
3. Separate flows from stocks; always chase the why.
4. The economic and market story must connect.
5. Surprise vs expectations, filtered through the policy quadrant.
6. Positioning and plumbing decide how violent the move is.
7. Study history: replay past crises (databases go back to 1918) through today's balance sheets.

## Glossary (short)
Capital · GDP · Balance sheet · **BoP** (ledger of all cross-border flows; backbone of FX) · Yield · Real yield · Breakevens · QE/QT · **STIR** (short-term rate market betting on CB moves) · Surprise index · Positioning · Repo · Liquidity · **COT** (weekly CFTC positioning report).

> `[Claude+]` **Applying this to your gold/FX system**: layers 1–3 = your weekly macro bias (Macro Tracker), layer 4 = the calendar/surprise filter before news, layer 5 = COT plus the liquidity/stop-hunt logic you already use in SMC. SMC "liquidity grabs" are a microstructure view of layer 5.

## Self-test
1. List the five layers in order with their typical timescale.
2. GDP identity? Which component dominates in the US and roughly how much?
3. Flow vs stock: classify GDP, household debt, the current account, government debt. → flow, stock, flow, stock
4. NFP +250k vs +200k consensus. Direction for 2y yields, USD, gold? → ↑, ↑, ↓ (typically)
5. Real yields falling while inflation expectations rise. Which quadrant, and which assets does it favour? → Easy money: gold, copper, equities, risk FX; USD soft
6. Why can good data plus crowded long positioning still produce a sell-off? → Nobody left to buy; any disappointment triggers exits
7. What is repo and why is it a warning light? → Overnight collateralised funding; stress there hits every leveraged market
8. Walk the labour → bonds transmission chain forwards and backwards.
