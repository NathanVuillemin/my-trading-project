# 00.4 · Positioning & Plumbing (Layer 5 deep dive)
Summary in my own words. `[Claude+]` = my additions.

## Key ideas
- **Plumbing** = how liquidity and collateral travel between balance sheets. QE is a relay: the Treasury auctions bonds → dealers buy them → the Fed buys from dealers with newly created reserves. QT reverses it. The goal is to know *which balance sheets* are involved when plumbing makes headlines.
- **COT routine**: long-term trend, 3-month trend and weekly change of net positioning; score it (z-score or rate of change) to spot extremes. Lesson from March 2023: specs were heavily short front-end Treasuries, the banking scare forced everyone to cover at once → violent move. *The more one-sided the crowd, the smaller the spark needed.*
- **Options: six dials** → expiry calendar (opex as catalyst), open interest/volume (dealer gamma hedging can push price), implied vs realised vol (the fear premium), vol term structure (inverted = event imminent), skew (tail pricing), sector option activity vs your regime view. Together they answer: *how is the vol surface changing?*
- **Fund flows** matter most at turning points and when they **contradict** COT/options (e.g. retail ETF inflows while futures specs sell and vol cheapens). Watch foreign flows too (e.g. Japanese buying of foreign bonds vs JPY and USTs).
- **Active vs passive vs systematic**: rules-based flows are constrained and leave price signatures (e.g. autocorrelation/trend persistence).
- **Signals need a why**: positioning only becomes tradeable on top of layers 1–4.
- **Microstructure**: three sessions (Asia, London, NY) with different players; intraday volume patterns; seasonality (only with a causal reason); order imbalances and liquidity depth. Simple starter kit: VWAP + session volume + scheduled releases as catalysts (→ 00.8).

> `[Claude+]` **COT for your markets**: gold = COMEX GC (use *Managed Money* in the Disaggregated report); FX = CME currency futures (use *Leveraged Funds* in TFF, or Non-Commercial in Legacy). Data is as of Tuesday, released Friday 15:30 ET, so it's always ~3 days stale. Practical signal: net spec position z-score over 3 years. Above +1.5 = crowded long (fragile to bad news); below −1.5 = crowded short (squeeze fuel). Remember that JPY futures are quoted as JPY/USD, so "long yen futures" = short USDJPY.

> `[Claude+]` **Link to SMC**: liquidity pools above highs and below lows are the microstructure version of "crowded positioning". Stops = forced orders. A sweep followed by displacement is the intraday analogue of a COT squeeze.
