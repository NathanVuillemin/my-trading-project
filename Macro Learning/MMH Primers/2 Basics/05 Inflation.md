# 05 · Inflation
Condensed summary in my own words. `[Claude+]` = my additions.

## Main lessons
- Inflation sets the **discount rate** for everything → bonds (mechanically), equities (via the multiple), gold (via real yields), FX (via rate differentials). 2022: earnings held, but stocks and bonds fell together (multiple compression, stock–bond correlation turned positive).
- **Hierarchy**: when inflation is quiet, markets trade growth; when it's high or moving fast, inflation takes over and growth news is read through it.
- **Measures**: CPI (fastest, most traded), core (trend), PCE/core PCE (the Fed's 2% target, usually a bit below CPI), PPI (pipeline, margins). **Composition beats headline.**
- **US CPI weights (approx.)**: shelter ~35%, services ex-shelter ~26%, goods ~19%, food ~13%, energy ~7%. Sticky: shelter and services. Flexible/noisy: energy and food. Shelter is measured with long lags.
- **Arithmetic**: YoY = 12 monthly changes chained → **base effects** are predictable. Use **3m or 6m annualised** MoM for the current pace.
- **Expectations** are self-fulfilling; keeping them anchored is the central bank's priority. Gauges: breakevens, **5y5y forward** (the purest anchoring gauge), surveys. Danger = markets suspecting the anchor is slipping.
- **Pipeline**: commodities (weeks) → PPI (months) → goods CPI (quarters) → services/wages (sticky). A commodity spike only becomes inflation if it survives into services and wages.
- **Four regimes (level × direction)**:
  | | Falling | Rising |
  |---|---|---|
  | High | Disinflation from a peak (2023): duration rallies, multiples expand, quality leads | Overheat/stagflation (2022): commodities, TIPS, cash; stocks and bonds both hurt |
  | Low | Deflation scare (2008, 2020): long bonds and gold; credit and cyclicals hurt | Reflation (2016–17, 2020–21): cyclicals, value, commodity FX; short duration |
- **Gold trades real yields, not inflation per se**: inflation + hawkish CB can sink gold; inflation that the CB tolerates sends it higher.
- Read the **reaction** to prints (00.7 matrix).

> `[Claude+]` **Annualising**: 3m annualised = ((1+m1)(1+m2)(1+m3))^4 − 1. Three core prints of 0.3% ≈ 3.7% annualised; 0.2% ≈ 2.4%. Rule of thumb: **0.17% MoM ≈ 2% annual**. A core CPI of 0.3% vs a 0.2% consensus is a meaningful hawkish surprise.

> `[Claude+]` **CPI day playbook for XAUUSD**: hot core (≥0.1 above consensus) → typical first move USD ↑, gold ↓ (real yields ↑). Watch whether breakevens or real yields do the moving (TIPS vs nominal). If breakevens lead and real yields stay flat, the gold dip often gets bought (inflation-hedge demand). Wait for the first 15–30 min liquidity sweep before applying SMC entries.
