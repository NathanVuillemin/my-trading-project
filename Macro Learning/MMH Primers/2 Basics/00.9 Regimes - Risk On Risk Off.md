# 00.9 · Reading the Market's Mood (Regimes)
Condensed notes in my own words. `[Claude+]` = my additions.

## Key points
- **"Bull vs bear" is a definitional game** (often marketing). The pro answer to "are we in a bull market?" is "on what horizon, and for what risk tolerance?" A regime exists relative to rules *you* chose and can test.
- **Simple regime rules** (MA, rolling returns, Bollinger bands) are fine, but lookback length changes signal frequency and quality: long = few flips, late; short = many flips, fake-outs.
- **Markov models**: states + transition probabilities; the next state depends only on the current one. You get odds of staying or switching rather than a binary label, which is what sizing needs. It's fuzzy, probabilistic thinking for noisy systems.
- **Stack definitions**: trend state (MA) × variance state (Markov switching) → 4 regimes: bull/low-vol (calm grind up), bull/high-vol (volatile rise), bear/low-vol (quiet range), bear/high-vol (real bear).
- **Correlations by regime**, not across all history: stocks, bonds, gold and oil behave differently in each.
- **RORO defined properly**: risk-off = a broad *expansion of risk premia across assets* at once, not just "stocks down". Full stack = Markov states (what) + regime correlation matrix (how it spreads) + risk-premia matrix (why).

> `[Claude+]` **A simple regime filter you could add to your system**:
> - Trend: price vs 50-day EMA on the daily (above = up).
> - Variance: 20-day ATR vs its 1-year median (above = high).
> - Four buckets → track your trade stats per bucket. SMC continuation setups usually work best in trend/low-vol; reversal setups in high-vol.
> - Risk proxy: VIX > 20 or HY spreads widening = risk-off → reduce size on risk FX (AUD, NZD); gold and JPY are candidates for longs.

> `[Claude+]` Hidden Markov models can be fitted in Python (`hmmlearn`) on daily returns with 2–3 states. That's a good future project for your backtest repo.
