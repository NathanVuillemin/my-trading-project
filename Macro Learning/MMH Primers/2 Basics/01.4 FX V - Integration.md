## Main lessons
- **A model = stacked if/then functions leading to an action**, not necessarily code. The course's example: CPI accelerating + curve pricing more hikes + upward-sloping breakevens → bearish bonds; if all three agree, short on an MA cross with a 3σ stop. Understand the variables *first*, then backtest.
- **Top-down questions**: how is the structural regime quantified, and how does it constrain the cyclical one? Continuity/discontinuity of structural, cyclical and their combination vs history → what skew does that create for this pair?
- **Bottom-up questions**: idiosyncratic drivers the regime map misses; changing correlations with other assets; how positioning shifts as information arrives.
- Track how information moves from uncertain → certain relative to preconditions (hard to backtest, very valuable).
- **Attribution always**: even with no forecast, know what's driving the pair *now*. Regime shifts = attribution shifts → offside positioning → implied vol spikes and nonlinear moves.
- **Positioning is always in the price**. The author argues COT and surveys have little predictive value for FX. Better reads: **implied vol** (real money at stake), shifting cross-asset correlations (e.g. bonds vs inverted DXY), and the size of GIP changes vs the size of price moves.
- **Catalyst matrix** (data above/in-line/below × price up/flat/down, in degrees): mismatches reveal positioning. Each release is a test of the thesis *and* a chance to enter. If attribution says the regime hasn't changed, a counter-move on data is an entry, not a refutation.
- **Quant overlay**: two families, **momentum** (CTA/trend) and **mean reversion**. Markets switch between them (bonds after March 2023: MR first, then downside momentum). Combine them for low correlation; tune to *your* constraints; beware over-optimisation. A typical pro setup: both families across timeframes, scaled sizing, std dev + momentum + D/W/M OHLC levels.
- The edge = how you **weigh the combination of variables**, plus information edge or a different time horizon or risk tolerance.
