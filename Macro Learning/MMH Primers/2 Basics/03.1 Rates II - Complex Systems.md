# 03.1 · Rates II — feedback loops and where alpha lives
Condensed summary in my own words. `[Claude+]` = my additions.

## Main lessons
- **Foundations over "arrival"**: the costliest setbacks come from wrong mental models. Many old edges are now cheap beta, so edge today needs a deeper map.
- **Dashboard**: 4 families (nominal, real, breakevens/inflation swaps, forward curve via SOFR futures/swaps) × (outrights at 3m/2y/5y/10y/30y, every curve pair, the curve-regime tag). Different parts of the curve can be in different regimes at once, and that divergence is information. Add rates vol (implied, skew, term structure), quant regime signals, and other countries later.
- **Inflation engine room**: CPI, PCE, PPI matter because the Fed uses them, markets react to them, contracts settle on CPI, and they nowcast each other. Apply MAs, rate of change and medians to headline, core and major line items; research the *line items* driving outsized moves. Speed matters (−20bp/month reaches target twice as fast as −10bp).
- **Transmission rule**: an inflation story must show up in the printed CPI, or it doesn't matter. That's why surveys move bonds less (hedges settle on CPI).
- **Everything shows up somewhere in rates**: inflation = release valve between nominal demand and real output. A useful gauge: fed funds − CPI (real policy stance) vs growth.
- **Catalysts**: CPI, PPI, PCE, NFP, FOMC, auctions. Run the 00.7 routine (surprise, σ-size of reaction, did it hold into the next high-volume period?). Don't bet on single prints (coin toss) unless the market is clearly offside *and* the print would falsify it. Most Fed speeches are noise; backtested patterns decay.
- **Complex adaptive system traits**: reflexivity, nonlinearity, feedback loops (amplifying vs dampening), time delays (→ overshoot), evolution/skew (S-curves, fat tails), path dependence (cycles are a description, not a law). Agents overweight the seen and certain → edge = correctly defined **asymmetry** in the unseen. Diversify across assets, strategies and timeframes.
- **Return decomposition**: Treasury returns = realised short rate (beta) + change in expected short rate (policy path) + term premium. **Alpha lives in the curve** (near-term labour/inflation → policy path; long-run NGDP/liquidity → term premium). The long end = short-end path + term premium.
- **Short-end trade**: 3m vs 2y against inflation direction, level and speed. 2y well below 3m = cuts priced. **Trade delivery vs pricing**: 6 cuts priced, 3 delivered → bonds can sell off *while the Fed cuts*.
- Inflation level and trend set bonds' momentum vs mean-reversion regime.
- **Know why you're paid**: hunt for near-certain pricing that's unlikely to be exactly right. Rates are ideal because contracts price Fed and inflation outcomes on fixed dates, so you can see where traders will be forced to change their minds.
- **Options precision**: delta (direction), gamma (speed), vega (size), theta (timing).
- Learn and trade the short end first; the long end is derived from it.

> `[Claude+]` **"Priced vs delivered" for FX**: the same logic drives currency reactions to central bank meetings. A 25bp cut when 50bp was priced = hawkish surprise = currency *up*. Always check the OIS/CME FedWatch pricing (or ECB/BoE equivalents) the day before.

> `[Claude+]` **Term premium data**: ACM term premium (NY Fed) and Kim-Wright (Fed Board, FRED `THREEFYTP10`). A rising term premium with a steady policy path = a bear steepener that often hurts both equities and gold short-term (higher real long yields), unless it's driven by fiscal/credibility fears, which can be gold-*bullish*.
