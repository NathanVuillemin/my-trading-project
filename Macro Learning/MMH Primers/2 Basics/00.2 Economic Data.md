## Main lessons
- **Cherry-picking trap**: the economy has so many series that you can "prove" almost any story. The fix is to look at *all* the data and **weight by importance** (consumption ≈ 2/3 of US GDP; a niche survey is not). Most bad macro calls are weighting errors.
- **Country = company report**: flow data is the income statement (→ growth and inflation); capital-structure data is the balance sheet (supported by liquidity). This is why macro tracks **G·I·P**: growth, inflation, policy.
- **Data map**: flows = GDP components, national income (profits, wages), labour, sectors, surveys, trade/BoP, prices. Stocks = sector balance sheets (look for **asset–liability mismatches**, e.g. short-term funding of long-term assets). Balance sheets are how flows get transmitted into markets.
- **Frequency trick**: monthly releases are noisy early versions of quarterly GDP lines → nowcasting. Monthly = speed; quarterly = quality. Connect each monthly series to its quarterly parent.
- **Circular flow**: one person's spending is another's income. Income = employees × hours × wages. The labour market is the hub; break it and spending follows.
- **Dataset cautions**: services prices are mostly wages · housing is rate-sensitive but *not* the whole business cycle (don't over-learn 2008) · government spending isn't automatically inflationary (does it fill a gap or overheat?) · PMIs count better/worse, not *how much* · never read price data in isolation from supply/demand.
- **Simple tools win**: moving averages (trend), rate of change (acceleration), standard deviation (is the move actually unusual?).
- **Routine**: list every dataset for the country → read the source descriptions → build a thesis that explains both agreeing *and* disagreeing data. Everything is free (FRED, regional Fed sites).
- The real edge of knowing the data: you know what is **not** happening.

> `[Claude+]` **Monthly → quarterly GDP mapping (US)**
> | Quarterly GDP line | Monthly clue |
> |---|---|
> | Consumption | Personal income & outlays (PCE spending), retail sales, auto sales |
> | Business investment | Core capital goods orders/shipments (durables report) |
> | Residential investment | Housing starts, permits, new/existing home sales |
> | Inventories | Business inventories, wholesale/retail inventories |
> | Net exports | Monthly trade balance (advance goods trade) |
> | Government | Monthly Treasury statement |
> Ready-made nowcasts: **Atlanta Fed GDPNow**, NY Fed Staff Nowcast.

> `[Claude+]` **Z-score** = (latest − mean) / std dev. |z| > 2 is genuinely unusual. A useful column for your Macro Tracker: z-score each release's *surprise* (actual − consensus) against its own history, so a 50k NFP miss and a 0.1% CPI miss become comparable.

> `[Claude+]` **Key US calendar for FX/gold**: NFP + AHE (1st Friday), CPI (~mid-month), PCE (~end of month), retail sales, ISM manufacturing/services (1st/3rd business day), jobless claims (Thursday), JOLTS, FOMC (8x/year), GDP advance (~4 weeks after quarter end).
