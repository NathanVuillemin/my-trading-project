# 04 · Growth
Condensed summary in my own words. `[Claude+]` = my additions.

## Main lessons
- Nearly every asset is a claim on future activity, so a change in expected growth reprices everything. Growth comes first in GIP; inflation and policy respond to it.
- **Trade direction and surprise, not level.** Three questions: accelerating or decelerating? Faster or slower than priced? Is growth or inflation the market's binding worry?
- **Cycle phases** (timing varies, sequence repeats): early (easy policy, cheap assets → equities, HY, cyclicals), mid (long, dull, trends and carry work, vol sold), late (hot, tight capacity, firm inflation → commodities, value), recession (duration and havens lead, spreads blow out).
- **Output gap** = actual − potential. It links growth to inflation. It's estimated, so use it as a compass, not a precise gauge.
- **Leading** (PMI new orders, permits, claims, expectations, yield curve) → **coincident** (NFP, IP, real retail sales, real income) → **lagging** (unemployment rate, core inflation, wages, delinquencies). Build the view from the leading edge and check against the coincident data. *Leaders rolling over while coincident data still looks fine* = the trade.
- **Second derivative** is where markets live: "bad → less bad" is a strong risk-on setup; "great → good" hurts. Scare vs recession: a scare stabilises; a recession self-reinforces via jobs and credit. Check breadth of deterioration and credit's reaction.
- **Transmission**: equities (acceleration → cyclicals, small caps, value; deceleration → defensives, quality, mega-caps), bonds (acceleration → higher yields/steeper; deceleration helps duration), credit (a growth instrument in bond form), FX/commodities (relative growth attracts capital; global acceleration lifts industrial metals and their currencies).
- **Twist**: good growth news is only good for risk when inflation isn't the constraint. Otherwise hot data = higher rates first.

> `[Claude+]` **Growth → FX/gold quick map**: US growth outperforming the rest of the world → USD up (the "US exceptionalism" trade) → headwind for gold. Global (esp. China) acceleration → AUD, NZD, CAD up, copper up. US slowdown with sticky inflation → worst for USD bonds, supportive for gold. A handy free relative-growth gauge is the Citi Economic Surprise Index spread (US minus G10 or EZ) vs EURUSD.

3. How do you tell a growth scare from a recession?
4. Name 2 leading, 2 coincident and 2 lagging indicators.
