# 01.3 · FX IV — The dollar world today (Part 4 of 5)

## Main lessons
- **The US is the worked example precisely because it's the exception.** Transfer the *checklist*, not the answers: Trinity position → BoP → dominant GIP impulse (with the 00.1 country profile as input).
- **US on the Trilemma**: no peg (compare the SAR's flat line against USD); **free capital** (a reason for reserve status, and why CNY's capital controls block it from becoming a reserve rival); **independent Fed** (mandate first, the dollar second). So the dollar floats and absorbs pressure.
- Pegs sit on a **spectrum** (hard fix → managed/strategic intervention like Japan and China → clean float), and a peg's strength depends on **FX reserves**.
- **Flow vs structure**: the current account = the flow (demand minus output; decades of US deficits → offshoring → supply-chain fragility). The balance sheet = the stock of claims foreigners accumulate in exchange (visible in the Fed's **Z.1 Financial Accounts** on FRED).
- **Countries can be over-leveraged like companies**; the failure mode is an **asset–liability mismatch**. Where the stress shows depends on the Trinity: reserves under a peg, the exchange rate under a float. For the USD, the constant foreign need for dollar assets cushions it.
- Cross-border balance-sheet overlap is why correlations and spillovers exist.
- **Impulse, not level**: markets trade the *accelerating phase* of a driver. 2022 = inflation impulse + Fed tightening dominated; 2023 = inflation still high but the impulse fading, a different regime. Identify the dominant impulse and how it moves risk premia across assets; watch for handoffs (e.g. to a negative growth impulse, then CB cuts as an offsetting input).
- **Net the two functions**: growth → earnings; liquidity → valuation. Growth down + easing up = a net, not a verdict.

> `[Claude+]` **Impulse in practice**: impulse ≈ the change in the YoY rate (or 3m annualised vs 12m). E.g. core CPI YoY 3.8% → 3.5% over 3 months = negative impulse even at a high level. A column in your Macro Tracker for "3m annualised minus YoY" per country gives you a live impulse sign for growth and inflation, and the *difference between two countries' impulses* is a clean input for pair bias.

> `[Claude+]` **Gold under this lens**: gold is effectively a currency with no issuer, so no Trinity and no BoP. Its "policy" is the Fed (via real yields and USD) plus central-bank reserve demand. The post-2022 reserve-diversification bid is a *structural* flow (slow, persistent), sitting on top of the cyclical real-yield driver.
