# 00.3 · From Data to Prices (Layer 3 deep dive)
Summary notes in my own words. `[Claude+]` = my additions.

## Main lessons
- **Bridge**: group assets by sector and sensitivity, then connect each group to the data that drives it (retail sales → retailers, IP → industrials, inflation → bonds). The result: you can read the economy through company results and the market through economic releases.
- **Equities = earnings × multiple**. Earnings run on the real economy; the multiple runs mainly on **liquidity**. 2022 was a multiple collapse, not an earnings collapse. Also think **cross-sectionally** (sector vs sector) and know your **factor** exposures (value, quality, momentum, low-vol), which behave differently by regime.
- **Fixed income = two daily questions**: what is duration doing, and what is credit doing?
  | | Spreads calm | Spreads widening |
  |---|---|---|
  | Yields rising | Pure rates/duration story | "Double trouble" (worst for risk) |
  | Yields falling | Calm rally / soft-landing | Flight to safety / recession scare |
- **Commodities aren't "inflation"**: they hit specific CPI components and only sometimes pass into core. Model each commodity's own supply/demand cycle.
- **FX = gateway**: to buy anything in a country you must first buy its currency. So ask *who is constrained to buy or sell it*, using the BoP as the visitor log. FX is hardest because you need everything else right, which is also why the opportunity is biggest.
- **Top-down vs bottom-up**: price G·I·P across all assets versus the asset's own fundamentals. Agreement = conviction; disagreement = either an edge or a mistake. Investigate.
- **Expectations routine**: sector surprise indices; YoY, MoM and 3-month trend for every series; triangulate earnings surprises, economic surprises, valuations and STIR repricing. The regime sets the *distribution* of likely outcomes.
- **Best trades**: fade what's unrealistically priced. Disconfirmation ("X can't do Y") is easier than predicting ("X will do Y").

> `[Claude+]` **Gold in the duration × credit grid**: gold tends to do best in the "yields falling" row, and especially the *flight to safety* cell (real yields ↓ + fear). It struggles in the "pure rates" cell (real yields ↑, no fear). In "double trouble" it's mixed: fear helps, but forced liquidation (margin calls) can hit gold early. See March 2020, when gold fell ~12% before rallying.

> `[Claude+]` **Fade checklist**: (1) What exactly is priced? (STIR/OIS for rates, implied vol for size of move.) (2) What must be true for that pricing to be right? (3) Is that plausible given the data trend? (4) Is positioning crowded in the priced direction? If there's a clear *no* at (3) plus yes at (4), you have a candidate fade.
