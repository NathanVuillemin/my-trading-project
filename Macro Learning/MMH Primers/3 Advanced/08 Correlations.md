# 08 · Correlations (stock–bond)
Condensed summary in my own words. `[Claude+]` = my additions.

## Main lessons
- **Why care**: relative moves turn one-dimensional signals into regime information; managers feel pain at the *portfolio* level (2022 hurt more than 2020 because both sleeves fell); every asset has its own G/I/L sensitivity, and correlation reveals the overlaps.
- **Driver**: the mix of growth × inflation × liquidity (each expanding or contracting → 8 scenarios, in varying strength) sets the stock–bond correlation. The course compares it to a three-body problem: estimate and update, don't "solve". Mapping nominal vs real GDP (level, rate of change, spread) informs credit and duration risk.
- **Rule**: a real correlation needs a **shared input driving both assets' attribution at the same time**, and that mapping expires as drivers rotate. If you can't name the input, treat it as a statistical artifact.
- **Mechanics**: inflation is always priced via swaps and breakevens (even when the Fed lags, as in 2021); the Fed's reaction to inflation (filtered through growth) shapes the curve, e.g. 2023's wide policy-minus-inflation spread was allowed by resilient growth; rates then feed into equities.
- **Two cases**: 2020 recession + deflation → negative correlation (bonds hedged). 2022 Fed liquidity drain → positive correlation (both fell), persisting into 2023–24 "Goldilocks" repricing off the same policy path.
- **Flow behind it**: vol-control and cross-collateralised portfolios de-risk *both* legs mechanically when correlation turns positive, and mechanical sellers don't stop at fair value.
- **Trades**: 8 building blocks (long/short each, the 2 relative pairs, long both, short both), vol-weighted. Long-both or short-both on a vol-weighted basis often has better odds than a single leg because you trade the regime. Even untraded, the correlation is a daily macro-cycle gauge. Blind 60/40 is over; active management is needed.

> `[Claude+]` **Gold correlations to track** (rolling 60-day on daily returns): XAU vs DXY (normally negative), XAU vs 10y TIPS yield (normally strongly negative; a weakening link = central-bank/haven demand dominating), XAU vs SPX (flips: positive in liquidity rallies, negative in fear). A sudden correlation break is itself a regime-change alert, so name the new shared input before trading it.
