# 23 · Gold
Condensed summary in my own words. `[Claude+]` = my additions. **Core module for your XAUUSD trading.**

## Core points
- Gold = money without an issuer: no credit risk, no policy-credibility risk. It shines when confidence in monetary/fiscal frameworks erodes.
- **Driver stack** (slow → fast):
  1. **Real rates** (10y TIPS): the opportunity cost of a zero-yield asset. Lower = supportive.
  2. **USD**: priced in dollars and a reserve alternative. Weaker USD = inflows.
  3. **Global liquidity + central-bank reserve diversification** (EM CBs moving away from USD; strategic, price-insensitive).
  4. **Flows**: ETFs, futures positioning, trend followers (set the short-term tempo).
  5. **Stress = convexity trigger**: big outperformance when trust in policy, institutions or capital mobility is *impaired*, not just when uncertainty ticks up.
- **Market structure**: London OTC (LBMA; bullion banks, CBs, refiners; ~400oz Good Delivery bars; forwards/swaps/unallocated) = the core liquidity pool. **COMEX futures** = leverage and price discovery. **EFP** links the two. Physically backed ETFs move London vault holdings. CBs buy bilaterally.
- **Five regimes**: 1971 float → 1970s surge to the 1980 peak (negative real rates, broken anchors) → 1990s slump to ~$250 (CBs selling; the 1999 agreement capped sales) → 2008–11 QE rally → post-2020 "credibility bid": CB buying persists **even with positive real yields**.
- **Stock, not flow**: ~220,000t above ground vs ~3,000–3,300t/yr mined (~1%). Supply can't respond, so price is set by **changes in ownership**. *Conviction buyers* (CBs, ETFs, macro funds; price-insensitive) set **direction**. Research cited: ~70% of monthly variation; ~100t net buying ≈ +1.7%. *Opportunistic buyers* (EM households; buy dips, slow on rallies) set **amplitude** and floor declines. So "high prices cure high prices" doesn't apply; gold falls hard only when *conviction* breaks.
- Explains the paradoxes: gold up with positive real yields, down in some inflation scares, up despite ETF outflows, whenever CBs are bidding.
- **Trade channels**: real-rate expressions (gold outperforms most when real yields fall for *bad* reasons: fiscal dominance, credibility); contango carry/curve (backwardation = rare physical squeeze); vol/skew (buy vol when implied is compressed despite macro risk); ETF vs spot/futures dislocations; miners (geared pure plays vs diversified majors; streamers/royalties behave like long-duration assets).
- **Participants**: CBs (floor), bullion banks (liquidity, lease rates, London–NY arbitrage), miners (hedge sellers on rallies, shaping the curve), ETFs, hedge funds/CTAs (momentum, reflexivity), retail (EM = savings, dip-buyers; DM = stress spikes).

> `[Claude+]` **Divergence tells**:
> - Gold up while real yields rise and DXY rises → the conviction bid (CBs/geopolitics) is dominant. Dips tend to get bought; don't short on real-yield logic alone.
> - Gold down while real yields fall → liquidation/dash for cash or conviction fading. Check VIX and margin-call conditions.
> - Gold in other currencies (XAUEUR, XAUJPY) at highs too → a genuine gold story, not just USD weakness.

> `[Claude+]` **Session behaviour worth knowing**: Asia (Shanghai premium, Chinese demand) often sets the early tone; the **LBMA auctions (10:30 & 15:00 London)** and **COMEX open (08:20 ET)** are liquidity windows; US data at 08:30 ET creates the classic sweep-and-reverse setups your SMC system targets. First-notice/roll days for COMEX active months (Feb, Apr, Jun, Aug, Oct, Dec) can distort futures vs spot.
