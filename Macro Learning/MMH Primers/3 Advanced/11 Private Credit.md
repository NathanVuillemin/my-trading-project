# 11 · Private Credit
Condensed summary in my own words. `[Claude+]` = my additions.

## Core points
- **What it is**: loans made and held by non-bank lenders (funds, BDCs), not publicly traded. Mostly **direct lending** (~half the asset class): senior secured, floating over SOFR, covenants, ~80% sponsor-backed (PE buyouts, add-ons, refis). It grew because post-GFC regulation pushed banks away from risky mid-market borrowers; now it competes with syndicated loans and HY, including $1bn+ "jumbo" deals.
- **Size (course figures)**: US ~$1.3tn, roughly 30% of sub-IG corporate debt issuance (vs ~13% post-GFC); global estimates vary widely depending on the definition.
- **Key quirk**: loans rarely trade, so marks are quarterly judgements. Low *observed* volatility ≠ low risk. **Price discovery happens in public proxies**: listed BDC NAV discounts, alt-manager stocks, software equities.
- **The chain**: LPs (pensions, insurers, SWFs, HNW; retail via BDCs with ~5%/quarter redemption caps) → concentrated managers → sponsor-backed borrowers (heavy software tilt) → with **banks wrapped around it** (fund-level lines, warehouse financing, leverage).
- **Why it worked**: ZIRP-era income hunger, quiet amend-and-extend by small lender clubs, low defaults, and software as "ideal" collateral (recurring revenue, high margins, sticky customers).
- **What changed**: higher rates plus AI making software economics more contestable (obsolescence, margin pressure). The course cites a ~30% software equity drop (Oct 2025–Feb 2026), BDCs −10%, the widest NAV discounts in 5+ years, weak fundraising, record redemption requests at some semi-liquid funds, and regulators (SEC, BoE, ECB, FCA) focusing on valuation and liquidity.
- **Stress path (mechanical)**: software equity doubts → public proxies reprice → refinancing tightens (fewer friendly rolls) → feedback via higher equity and funding costs. Gates reduce fire sales but teach investors that liquidity is conditional.
- **Base case**: dispersion by manager quality and sector, not a 2008-style event. The credit cycle still exists; it's concentrated in less visible vehicles.

> `[Claude+]` **Relevance to your FX/gold book**: a private-credit wobble would most likely show up as (1) wider HY spreads, (2) weaker regional banks and alt managers, (3) risk-off FX (JPY/CHF bid, AUD/NZD offered), and (4) a potential gold bid if it triggers Fed-cut expectations. Proxy watchlist: BIZD (BDC ETF), alt managers (e.g. BX, APO, ARES, OWL), regional banks (KRE), HY OAS on FRED (`BAMLH0A0HYM2`).
