# 10 · Combining Edges
Condensed summary in my own words. `[Claude+]` = my additions.

## Main lessons
- **Idea 1, edge = detection, not prediction**: find when a participant has misread the system and is *constrained to unwind*. Without trading-pit faces to read, detection is quantitative: model all variables, in all combinations, over time.
- **Idea 2, patience vs impatience**: impatience shows up in overpaid hedges, capitulation and irrational premiums. Take the other side with a *different time preference and risk tolerance*, or you get shaken out with the crowd.
- **Process**: precision in process is what makes leverage safe. Discretion is the last step of a process, used where systematic models fall short. Each module can become spreadsheet inputs; AI can run routine steps. Build the **factory** (method for producing strategies), not the car.
- **"Pages in the book"**: stack independent reasons (GIP regime, credit/duration read, correlation structure, crowded/impatient positioning, catalyst on the calendar) → positive asymmetry that wins several ways. Always ask: *is this the best trade available, and what does it add to the portfolio?*
- **80/20 of competency**: getting big things right (80%) earns a seat at the table; the last 20% needs big *and* small things right, with no margin for error. Most people stop there.
- **Case: Sugar Myojin (Salomon, 1985)**: researched the new JGB futures before launch, judged them overpriced, sold futures and bought the bonds: research → spot the overpaying crowd → take the other side → wait.

> `[Claude+]` **A "pages" scorecard for your trades** (score 0/1 each, take trades ≥ 4/6):
